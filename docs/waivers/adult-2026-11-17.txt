PART 3 — KNOWN RISKS OF BLACKSMITHING
Blacksmithing involves fire, extreme heat, and the use of heavy hand tools. Some risks exist in every blacksmithing environment and cannot be completely eliminated, even with proper safety precautions. These known, inherent risks include, but are not limited to:
• Burns from hot metal, open flame, sparks, or forge equipment
• Eye injuries from sparks, flying metal fragments, or scale
• Cuts, bruises, or puncture wounds from hand tools, hammers, tongs, or sharp metal edges
• Inhalation of smoke, fumes, or particulates from the forge or heated materials
• Heat stress, heat exhaustion, or dehydration from working near high-temperature equipment
• Muscle strain, sprains, or overexertion from hammering and physical labor
• Trips, slips, or falls in a workshop environment containing tools and equipment
• Hearing effects from repeated hammer strikes and forge noise
• Injury from the unexpected or uncontrolled movement of hot metal or tools
I acknowledge that this list is not exhaustive. Additional risks may exist that are not listed above.
